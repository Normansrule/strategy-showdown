How the simulation works

Nothing on this page is real. The eight instruments are invented and carry a .SIM suffix; their prices come from a random model that runs in your browser, one tick per simulated second, from the same seed every time unless you type reset <seed>.

Each instrument's log return is a market factor times its beta plus its own shock. Both the factor and the shocks have GARCH(1,1) variance (Bollerslev 1986), so calm and turbulent stretches cluster and large moves are more common than a normal distribution would give, two of the stylized facts on How markets move. Bid and ask sit on a one-cent grid around the mid and widen when volatility rises. Trades arrive at random, more often when the market is busy; a buyer pays the ask, a seller hits the bid. Depth beyond the touch is drawn at random for each tick.

Paper orders never leave this tab and vanish when you close it. A market order walks the simulated depth, so larger orders get worse average prices. A limit order that crosses takes what the book shows inside its limit and rests the rest; a resting limit fills in full at the touch once the touch reaches it. There is no queue position, no market impact after a fill, no fees and no short-sale constraint, so paper results here are more favourable than real trading would be. Limit prices more than 10% from the mid are refused as likely typos.

The layout and keyboard workflow are in the spirit of professional trading screens. The command names are this project's own and the page is not affiliated with or endorsed by any terminal vendor or exchange.
